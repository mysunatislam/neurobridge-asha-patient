Motivation: eye-tracking AAC devices typically max out around 10–20 words/min and fatigue easily under long-term use; implantable BCIs solve the mobility gap but require surgery. FingerSpeak targets patients with ALS, spinal cord injury, or stroke who retain some low-effort, voluntary finger movement. A gesture must be held stably to confirm, and the hand must return to Rest before another phrase can trigger — this is deliberate: it trades a little speed for far fewer accidental or repeated activations, which matters more for a real AAC device than raw responsiveness. This is a portfolio prototype, not a validated clinical device — see README.md for the roadmap.

Train model

Each real sample is expanded into augmented variants (angle, timing, and tracking-noise jitter applied to the raw landmarks, before feature extraction) for robustness. Features are rotation-normalized 3D coordinates + finger-joint angles + fingertip distances + motion velocity (~98 per frame). A bidirectional GRU trains with early stopping on a held-out split.

Speak mode

Once trained, hold or perform a calibrated gesture until the ring around the video fills — that's the confirmation dwell time, longer for gestures like "Emergency." After it speaks, you must return to Rest before triggering again. A prediction is rejected outright — regardless of confidence — if it's too far from every known gesture, which catches confident-but-wrong guesses on movements the model was never calibrated on.
